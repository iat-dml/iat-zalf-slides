# Writing for IAT and ZALF slides

The audience is scientists, or people who work with scientists. Write the way a careful
researcher presents: precise, plain, and no more confident than the evidence. This applies to
both languages.

## Titles

A content slide's title states what the slide shows, as a short sentence in sentence case.

| Instead of | Write |
|---|---|
| Results | Yield variance doubled on sandy sites after 2022 |
| Our approach | Three living labs share one sampling protocol |
| Datenmanagement | Alle Projektdaten liegen seit Mai im gemeinsamen Katalog |
| The challenge | 14 of 31 projects have no data management plan |

Topic labels are right for dividers and for descriptive slides ("Study area", "Methoden").
No full stop at the end of a title. The closing line from the template is the one exception.

Keep titles to what fits the title area: about 60 characters on one line, two lines at most.

## Body text

- One point per slide. If a slide needs a second title, it is two slides.
- Fragments are fine in bullets; keep them parallel. Three to five bullets is plenty; one
  sentence beside a figure is often enough.
- Numbers carry a unit, and where it matters n, the period and the uncertainty: "42 plots",
  "0.21 m³ m⁻³ (SD 0.04)", "2019 to 2024".
- Cite on the slide where a figure, dataset or claim comes from: author and year, or the
  dataset name, in the source line. Full references can go on a final slide.
- Match the strength of the wording to the evidence. "X reduced Y by 12 %" for a measured
  effect, "X was associated with" for a correlation, "we expect" for a plan. One hedge, not a
  stack of them; no superlatives.
- Use the project's own terms consistently. Take them from the context files (and from the
  IAT background skill when it is available). Define an abbreviation at first use unless the
  whole audience uses it daily.
- Detail the speaker will say aloud goes in the speaker notes, not on the slide.

Never supply a number, citation, name or date that is not in the source material. Where the
deck needs one and it is missing, write `[TO CONFIRM: what is needed]` on the slide and list
all such markers for the user at the end.

## English

- British spelling, as in ZALF's own usage: Centre, modelling, visualisation, programme,
  organise.
- Sentence case for all headings.
- "living lab" in lower case unless part of a name; "Living Lab Explorer" as a product name.
- Decimal point, thin or no thousands separator in running text ("12 400 ha" or "12,400 ha",
  consistently), a space before units, "%" closed up or spaced consistently.

## German

- Sachlicher Stil, Sie-Form wo eine Anrede nötig ist. Keine Werbesprache.
- Etablierte deutsche Fachbegriffe statt Anglizismen: Reallabor (für living lab, sofern das
  Projekt es so verwendet), Forschungsdatenmanagement, Datenmanagementplan, Arbeitsgruppe.
  Englische Eigennamen und Produktnamen bleiben englisch.
- Überschriften in normaler Groß- und Kleinschreibung, kein Punkt am Ende.
- Dezimalkomma, Leerzeichen vor Einheit und Prozentzeichen ("12,5 %", "3 ha"), Datum als
  TT.MM.JJJJ, Abkürzungen mit Leerzeichen ("z. B.", "d. h.").
- Anführungszeichen „so“; Gedankenstrich als Halbgeviertstrich mit Leerzeichen, sparsam.
- Geschlechtergerechte Sprache: der Konvention des Projekts folgen, wie sie in den
  Kontextdateien steht. Gibt es keine, neutrale Formen verwenden (Forschende, Mitarbeitende,
  Projektleitung) und nicht eigenmächtig Sonderzeichen einführen.
- Lange Komposita in Titeln vermeiden, wenn sie die Titelzeile sprengen; lieber umstellen.

## Shape of the deck

Decide what the talk is for before outlining: to report a result, to get a decision, to
explain a service, to invite collaboration. Then:

- **Research talk:** question, why it matters here, approach, results (most of the slides),
  what follows from them, open points.
- **Status or committee talk:** what was done, what was found or delivered, what is open,
  what is needed from the audience.
- **External overview:** who the work is for, what it offers, one or two concrete examples,
  how to get involved.

Size it to the slot: about one slide per one and a half to two minutes, fewer if figures need
explaining. Leave out the agenda slide in talks under about fifteen minutes.
